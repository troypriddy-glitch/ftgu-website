What We Look For

Problems a Standard Inspection Cannot Find.

The main sewer line runs from the house to the city connection — typically 4 to 6 inches underground, fully inaccessible without a camera. Southeast Texas clay soils, mature trees, and older pipe materials create unique conditions that make sewer scope essential.

🌿

Root Intrusion

Tree roots follow moisture directly into sewer lines through joints and cracks. Southeast Texas has abundant mature oaks and pines whose root systems aggressively target sewer lines. Left untreated, roots cause complete blockage and pipe damage.

📉

Pipe Bellying & Sags

Clay soil movement and soil settling cause low spots — "bellies" — in sewer lines where waste accumulates and eventually causes chronic backups. Common in older SETX neighborhoods with expansive clay soils.

💔

Cracks & Collapses

Older clay tile, concrete, or cast iron pipe deteriorates over time. Cracks allow soil infiltration. Partial or full collapses create immediate backup risk and require urgent repair or replacement.

🔗

Pipe Offsets & Separations

Soil movement causes pipe joints to shift and separate. These gaps allow sewage to escape into the soil — a health hazard — and allow soil to enter the pipe, reducing flow capacity.

🏚️

Aging Pipe Materials

Homes built before 1980 frequently have clay tile or cast iron sewer lines near end of life. Identifying the pipe material helps you understand what condition to expect and what timeline for replacement may be realistic.

🚫

Improper Connections

Illegal connections, improper vent configurations, and non-code connections to the municipal system are identified and documented — important for buyers and for lender-required due diligence.
